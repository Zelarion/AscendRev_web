# Privacy and Terms page implementation review

Reviewed 29 September 2026 against the local website source and enquiry handler. This is a factual consistency review, not legal advice.

## Privacy Policy

| Statement | Source checked | Finding / action |
|---|---|---|
| Responsible organization, address, and privacy contact | `src/content/site.ts` | Matches the current site constants: AscendRev Outsourcing Services Corp., Calgary, and the listed email. |
| Form fields, optional comments, and honeypot | `src/components/sections/contact/ContactEnquiryForm.tsx`, `src/lib/enquirySchema.ts`, `public/api/enquiry.php` | Matches the form and server validation. The form asks for name, business email, entity name, phone, and optional comments; the hidden anti-spam field is not a visitor-facing question. |
| IP-derived data | `public/api/enquiry.php` | The handler reads `REMOTE_ADDR`, uses a full SHA-256 value for rate-limit filenames, and records the first 16 hex characters of a SHA-256 value with accepted enquiries. The previous claim that the hash could not be reversed was too absolute. The policy now says the value is derived personal information and does not represent the raw IP address in the enquiry row. |
| Analytics, ads, pixels, and request logs | Source search across `src/` and `public/` | No analytics service, ad pixel, or social pixel was found. The previous statement that AscendRev could not know visit or page data was too broad because the web host may process operational request logs. The policy now separates site tracking from possible hosting logs. |
| Cookies and browser storage | `src/lib/theme.ts`, `src/components/ui/PageLoader.tsx`, `src/app/layout.tsx` | No cookie API was found. The theme preference uses `localStorage`; the loader uses `sessionStorage`. The policy now discloses both local browser values and says they are not submitted with the form. |
| Enquiry storage and email | `public/api/enquiry.php` | The source writes the CSV outside the document root by default and sends mail through configured SMTP. The policy now identifies web and email providers as service processors. The live environment variables and actual provider configuration were not inspected. |
| Access from the Philippines | `src/content/site.ts`, `src/content/advantage.ts` | Consistent with the site’s declared Philippines operation and delivery model. |
| Retention and deletion | `public/api/enquiry.php` | The handler has no automatic enquiry-retention purge. The page describes the organization’s retention commitment; it does not promise automatic deletion. Actual manual retention practice was not verifiable from source. |
| Encryption, storage access, and breach response | `public/api/enquiry.php`, site configuration; Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada; National Privacy Commission of the Philippines | The policy limits the transit-encryption statement to HTTPS submissions and describes storage outside the public directory as the handler's default. The handler allows deployment-time storage-path overrides, so the production path is unverified. File permissions, TLS enforcement, and operational access controls were not verified because hosting access was expressly out of scope. The access restriction is an organizational commitment, not something this PHP source enforces. The breach-notice statement now refers to the applicable law instead of naming only Canada, because the site describes access from the Philippines and Philippine law provides notification duties in qualifying cases. See [OPC breach guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/business-privacy/safeguards-and-breaches/privacy-breaches/respond-to-a-privacy-breach-at-your-business/) and the [Philippine NPC breach-reporting guidance](https://privacy.gov.ph/pips-and-pics/breach-reporting/). Confirm the production setup before relying on the operational claims. |
| Access requests and complaints | Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada guidance; National Privacy Commission of the Philippines | The 30-day deadline now accounts for legally permitted extensions and notice. The OPC guidance says organizations covered by PIPEDA generally respond within 30 calendar days, subject to specified extensions. Since the policy discloses that teams in the Philippines may access enquiry information, it now names both privacy regulators as possible complaint channels, depending on the circumstances. See [OPC access-request guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/accessing-personal-information/obligations-for-organizations/02_05_d_54_ati_02/), [OPC report a concern](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/report-a-concern/), and the [Philippine NPC complaint process](https://privacy.gov.ph/file-a-complaint-2/). |

## Terms of Use

| Statement | Source checked | Finding / action |
|---|---|---|
| Website is informational; no accounts, checkout, or visitor publishing | `src/app/`, `src/components/`, `src/content/nav.ts` | Matches the implemented routes and controls. The old statement that there was “nothing to download” was not precise because browser-accessible site assets can be downloaded; the unnecessary claim was removed. |
| Enquiry form does not create a services engagement | `ContactEnquiryForm.tsx`, `public/api/enquiry.php` | Form submits an enquiry; the site does not accept payment or sign a services agreement. The claim about later engagement terms remains a business/legal term rather than a behavior the form can prove. |
| Rate limiting | `public/api/enquiry.php` | Matches the per-IP submission rate limiter. Its limit is configurable by environment; the policy does not publish a fixed number. |
| Email alternative when the form is unavailable | `src/content/site.ts`, Terms page | The contact email is configured. “Nothing is lost” was ambiguous and has been replaced with a statement that the conversation can continue by email. |
| Privacy link, external links, ownership, and availability | Routes, footer links, and site source | Internal privacy route exists; external profile/social links are links rather than embedded account or publishing tools. Ownership and uptime statements are policy/legal terms, not facts that can be proven from application code. |
| Liability, consumer rights, and Alberta law | Terms page; Government of Alberta consumer guidance | The consumer-rights carve-out is consistent with Alberta guidance that consumer protections under the Consumer Protection Act cannot be waived. The clause’s enforceability and choice of law depend on context; no legal opinion was made. See [Alberta’s Consumer Bill of Rights](https://www.alberta.ca/consumer-bill-of-rights) and [unfair business practices](https://www.alberta.ca/unfair-business-practices.aspx). |

## Production verification still unavailable

The review used local source only. BRC Host and cPanel were not accessed. The actual deployment’s storage path, SMTP provider and recipients, TLS enforcement, server-log retention, and access permissions therefore remain unverified. The policy avoids claiming that AscendRev itself runs analytics, but it now acknowledges that the hosting provider may process operational request data.
